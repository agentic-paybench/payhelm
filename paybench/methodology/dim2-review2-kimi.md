## 1. Severity-Ranked Objections

<details>
<summary><b>O1. Ontological Incommensurability — FATAL to construct validity</b></summary>

The dimension assumes “authorization” is a single latent construct measurable in seconds across six rails. It is not. L402’s macaroon is a <i>grant-to-pay</i> (the server delegates permission before the client commits funds); <code>x402 /verify</code> and MPP <code>Verify</code> are <i>validations-of-payment</i> (the server checks payer-supplied credentials); AP2’s mandate step is an <i>orchestration hand-off</i> to an external settlement layer that never itself settles. These are different business events with incompatible risk transfers and counterparty obligations. The ISO 8583 analogy fails because MTI 0110 is globally standardized as “issuer approves and guarantees settlement subject to clearing”; PayBench’s six checkpoints share no such standardized commitment—funds are not cryptographically reserved in L402, and AP2 explicitly disclaims settlement. Naming them with trust/equivalence classes does not render the time intervals comparable, any more than labeling “triage” and “intake” as equivalent healthcare checkpoints makes their durations interchangeable. The scalar leaderboard is therefore measuring a chimera.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>O2. Statistical Cargo-Culting: Bradley-Terry on Timed Events — FATAL to ranking inference</b></summary>

Reusing Dimension 1’s Bradley-Terry MLE + <code>pass@k</code> + Wilson LB pipeline on authorization latency is a category error. BT requires binary pairwise preferences generated by a stable, transitive latent “strength” parameter. Latency distributions here are continuous, right-skewed, and heteroscedastic; heavy tails or variance differences can induce non-transitive cycles (e.g., Rail A usually beats B, B usually beats C, but C beats A when A suffers tail latency). Wilson lower-bound CIs are binomial confidence intervals; they ignore censoring (failed authorizations never observed within $k$ seconds) and assume independent Bernoulli trials. The correct tool for pairwise time-to-event racing is a stratified Cox model, Aalen-Johansen for competing risks, or a mixed-effects model on log-latency. Using BT + Wilson here is statistical cargo-culting that will produce pathological rankings and false confidence.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>O3. Instrumentation Artifact & Deployment Topology Confound — FATAL to external validity; SURVIVABLE only as a pure protocol micro-benchmark</b></summary>

For <code>x402</code> and MPP, the benchmark mandates explicitly calling internal <code>/verify</code> endpoints that fused deployments collapse into a single client-facing <code>/settle</code>. The measured checkpoint is therefore often invisible to the agent and subject to deployment topology variance: an in-process sidecar may yield sub-millisecond times, while a disaggregated microservice yields tens of milliseconds, even though the underlying rail protocol is identical. PayBench does not prescribe a reference facilitator topology (CPU pinning, network namespace, colocation rules), so scores confound rail choice with DevOps configuration. If PayBench claims to measure A2A rails, it must measure agent-observable latency; if it claims to measure protocol primitives, it must publish a strict reference topology. As designed, it does neither.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>O4. Temporal Causal Asymmetry (Process-Offset Confound) — FATAL to interval validity [NEW]</b></summary>

The clock start—“payment intent presented to rail”—denotes causally distinct lifecycle phases. In <code>x402</code>, MPP, and AP2, the client presents a fully-formed payment proposal and awaits reactive validation. In L402, the client requests a challenge and receives a macaroon <i>before</i> forming or committing to a payment intent. The benchmark therefore times pre-payment server overhead (menu generation) for L402 and in-payment validation overhead for the others. This is analogous to timing restaurant “order confirmation” against “time to receive the wine list” and calling both “authorization.” The interval boundaries are offset by a full causal stage, making the wall-clock differences uninterpretable as a single performance dimension.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>O5. Authorization Gaming via Null-Verify — SURVIVABLE under adversarial audit; otherwise SEVERE</b></summary>

Because the benchmark defines the checkpoint formally but not the <i>work</i> that must precede it, a benchmark-gaming facilitator can emit <code>/verify</code> immediately after parsing the request, deferring signature verification, balance checks, and risk scoring to <code>/settle</code>. The L402 macaroon can be pre-generated or cached. AP2 can issue a credential before mandate verification completes. Without a standardized proof-of-work requirement (e.g., cryptographic verification and fresh balance state must be included in the timed response), the leaderboard optimizes for API response latency, not genuine payment authorization. Goodhart’s Law applies ruthlessly.
</details>

## 2. New High-Severity Flaws (Outside G1–G7)

If G3–G7 cover operational or procedural issues, the following two structural flaws likely sit outside them:

- **Competing-risks censoring is unmodeled.** The benchmark does not specify how authorization *failures* (rejection, timeout, insufficient funds) are entered into the race. If failures are dropped, the BT likelihood conditions on success and flatters risky rails. If coded as $\infty$, they dominate <code>pass@k</code>. A valid benchmark must treat authorization as a competing-risk process (authorize vs. reject vs. timeout), not a guaranteed-finish race.
- **Ties are ignored by the BT specification.** Pairwise wall-clock races on networked sub-second services will generate frequent ties (differences within clock jitter). Standard BT MLE cannot handle ties without a Davidson extension. Ignoring them inflates pairwise variance and systematically biases ability estimates downward for consistent, low-jitter rails.

## 3. Adjudication

**DA1 — Vote: (b) CLIENT-ONLY**

A2A benchmarking must reflect agent-observable state transitions. An “authorization” that never crosses the agent-facing wire is an internal implementation detail, not a latency that shapes agent behavior or user experience. By restricting Dimension 2 to rails that natively emit a pre-settle accept to the client, PayBench enforces a sharp, auditable, and ecologically valid criterion. This disqualifies fused <code>x402</code> topologies and fused MPP Charge from this dimension, but that is a feature: it prevents conflating DevOps topology with protocol performance, and it aligns the metric with the ISO 8583 spirit (the agent sees the authorization response). Rails that fuse the steps can still be characterized by the accept→finalized gap published alongside Dimension 1.

**DA2 — Vote: (a) KEEP**

Forcing L402 into a synthetic “payment-accepted” signal (REPIN-R11) would fabricate a checkpoint that does not exist in the protocol, re-introducing exactly the instrumentation sin that CLIENT-ONLY seeks to eliminate. SPLITting grant-type and verify-type rails would leave the grant league with $n \approx 1$ (L402), obliterating the statistical power of pairwise estimation. The per-rail-canonical doctrine, upheld 3–1 in Dimension 1, is the least-worst option: it makes each rail’s native semantics transparent via trust/equivalence classes without destroying statistical power or inventing protocol state. The ontological critique (O1) remains valid, but it is better managed by explicit metadata annotations and bootstrap sensitivity analyses than by Balkanizing the field.

---

## 4. One-Line Net Verdict

**Verdict:** Dimension 2 is pre-registerable only if DA1 adopts CLIENT-ONLY (eliminating fused-topology artifact), DA2 retains per-rail-canonical points with mandatory equivalence-class footnotes, and the scoring stack replaces the mis-specified Bradley-Terry+Wilson machinery with a stratified survival model (e.g., Cox proportional hazards with ties) that handles censored failures—otherwise the published rankings will be constructually invalid and statistically uninterpretable.
